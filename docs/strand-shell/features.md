# Strand shell

What the shell in `dot_config/strand/` does. Parked Strand problems are in `strand-issues.md`.

## Principles

- One island, keyboard first and minimal. It replaces the bar and the hover popouts. (2026-11-04: three islands, then a full-width bar, plain, pill-shaped and glass, were tried and dropped; the owner preferred this one. They are in the history from `ce41a0f` to `47a7acc`.)
- The island never changes size. Its resting size has room for anything that goes in it.
- The launcher opens from the island, with keyboard navigation to every module worth keeping.
- Volume and brightness changes show on the island itself, not in a separate OSD.
- Layout: workspaces left, status and clock right.
- Theme: `theme-switch` stays in charge. A `from file` service reads `active.json` and `palette(...)` maps its names to Strand's roles, so every `$` token and built-in widget follows it and springs on a swap. Falls back to `from`'s colours if the file is missing or broken. Wallpaper still to come.

## What's built

- Launcher (`strand/launcher.strand`): grows out of the island on the focused monitor: it widens, then drops down, and closes the other way round, rolling up and narrowing back into the island (a click away is the one close that just fades). Just the menu of pages when it opens (apps come up as you type, or under Apps), typing searches everything, `=` starts a sum for `qalc` (Enter copies the answer). Pages for apps, workspaces (each in use by number, with its apps' icons, its main window's title and your monitors as small tiles with its own lit; Enter goes there, typing finds a workspace by what is open on it, and a preview beside the list shows it as it is: its monitor in proportion with the wallpaper there and each window live where it sits, the focused one ringed, beside a list of its apps), Wi-Fi (join, leave, radio on and off; a secured network not saved asks its password in place of the search, Del forgets a saved one, a "Hidden network" row joins one by name), Bluetooth (connect, pair nearby, power; a device pairing that asks for a PIN, a passkey or a code to confirm opens the launcher here with the question in place of the search, Enter answers, Esc refuses), sound (output and microphone: each one's live wave, mute with Enter; every row, device or program, with its level as a figure and a bar set with the arrows (or a scroll on the output and microphone); the device in use first, in the accent, headphones and monitor speakers by their own symbol; each program playing or recording, with its icon, moved to the next device with Enter; a Bluetooth headset's microphone wakes on its own while an app records, and the row says so until then), clipboard history (Enter copies), notifications (below), drives (listed while one is plugged in: Enter mounts or unmounts a filesystem, a mounted one showing its path and how full it is; an Eject row makes the drive safe to pull, says "ejecting" meanwhile and "Safe to remove" in the hint row once it is), themes (theme-switch's list with six colours each, opening on the theme in use; the whole shell takes the colours of the theme you move over until Esc or Enter, and a preview beside the list shows the wallpaper it would bring and its palette) media (every player, the playing first, opening on the one the island follows: its art, title and player; a card beside the list with the art (16:9 for a video), title, artist and album, shuffle, 15 s back, previous, play or pause, next, 15 s on and repeat (shuffle and repeat only where the player has them), the time with a scrubber, and the art as a soft wash behind; Enter plays or pauses the selected one, pausing the others it would play over, and the island follows it; the arrows change track and with Shift skip 15 s; a row after them sets the leading player's own volume) and wallpapers (those `wallpapers.json` gives the theme in use, with thumbnails, and a preview of the selected one on your monitors as they're laid out; Enter puts one up with awww, a set one image per monitor left to right; a theme switched anywhere keeps the wallpaper if it suits the new theme, else puts up the one `wallpapers.json` names for it) tray (listed while an app has a tray item: each app with its icon, its tooltip and an orange dot while it wants you; Enter is its own click, → opens its menu right there as rows, check marks, radio buttons, shortcuts and a submenu one deep included, ← or Esc going back out; an app that is only a menu opens it here too) and power (the power profile as one row with a segmented control, the lit segment sliding, Enter or the arrows moving it; the battery with its last three hours as a graph, the time left and its charge; then lock, suspend, restart and shut down, the last three asking for Enter again within three seconds, a red line draining under the ask). Workspaces, clipboard, notifications, themes and wallpapers widen to show the selected row beside the list, on a card read from the top: a copied text or image whole with its size and time (Delete removes it from the history); a notification's app, time, whole message and its buttons (Alt+1 to 3, Del, or a click), and with none selected a line for each app with its count and newest summary; a theme's wallpaper on your monitors (named) and its palette as a ramp of neutrals and its accents. Wi-Fi and Bluetooth stay narrow and carry it in the rows: the network joined or the devices connected first, each with its signal or battery as a figure and a short bar, the page's state on its switch row ("on · Meniscus · 78%"). Names and prose are in IBM Plex Sans, figures, times and code in JetBrains Mono; the selection is a wash of the accent eased out from the left edge, with a glowing rail the row's height and a faint sheen drifting slowly across it, gliding from row to row under the list as the selection moves (the selected row steps right off the rail), and what's connected, on or in use takes the accent too. The hint row lists Enter, Esc and the page's own keys; each page's row on the home list ends in its Alt letter as keycaps, lit while Alt is held; on the other pages holding Alt crossfades the hint row into each page's icon and letter (Tray, Media, Drives and Thunderbolt only while they have something); Alt+A, W, N, B, S, C, I, T, L, Y, R, M, D, H, K and P jump to them (a click on a letter too); Esc clears, goes back, then closes. The Keys page (Alt+K, `launcher.keys_key`) lists Hyprland's own binds, read from `hyprctl binds -j` so it never drifts from them: each bind's description in hyprland.lua, "Group: what it does", puts it in a group (Shell, Apps, Windows, Arrange, Workspaces, Sound, Screen; one without a description under Other), binds sharing a description are one line (1 to 0 as one range, the four arrows as one key), and the list holds the groups while the pane beside it shows the selected group's lines with their keys as keycaps; a search lists the lines that match, the pane showing the selected one's group with it lit. The mouse does the same: rows and the hint row's letters click, a click away closes it, and so does leaving it for a second when it was opened with a click and nothing typed since (opened from the keyboard, it stays). On the island anything but a workspace opens it; the Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, volume and drive symbols open their page, and a scroll on the volume symbol sets it.
- The island (`strand/island.strand`): 640×34 (the launcher's width, so it only drops out of it) with 12 px squircle corners (the launcher and a notification keep the same corners as they grow out of it, so only the size changes, the shadow deepening as the launcher drops); that monitor's workspaces as numbered chips (each numbered slot the compositor gives it, empty ones dim) with the icons of the apps on each (each app once, largest window first, up to three, an order that stays put as focus moves; an app that comes widens the chip, its number staying where it is), one accent highlight gliding to the one on screen, a workspace asking for attention turning orange with one pop of its number, a click going there and a scroll stepping through them; a workspace that comes or goes grows or shrinks its chip on the highlight's own spring, so nothing jumps under it; status symbols (Wi-Fi, volume, battery, its figure when under 30 % or charging, plus, to their left so they never move them, a Bluetooth device connected, DND, muted mic, a drive plugged in and a tray app that wants you, by its own icon, while true; the muted mic opens the Sound page, the tray app the Tray page), the date and the clock (its digits rolling on the minute); while something plays and is heard, a live line in the accent, from the bass (left) to the highs (right), its ends fading out, laid over the island out of its flow so nothing moves for it, filling the room between the workspaces and the symbols (14 px short of each) and following them as they change, and shown only while the workspaces and symbols leave it room (only shown: a click goes through it to the island and opens the launcher; a track that comes on its own nudges it), and a track you skip to with the media keys (`strand toggle island.play_key`, `next_key`, `prev_key`) shows for a moment with its art, title and artist; and volume, the microphone's level, brightness and the keyboard's light on every monitor: when one changes, everything else fades away and the island shows a symbol, a centred bar and the level as a figure (`strand/island.strand`).
- Notifications (`strand/notify.strand`): Strand is the notification server. A notification is the island growing down into a card of its width (640) on the focused monitor: the sender's icon (a bell when it has none), the summary and one line of the body, in IBM Plex Sans. A new one takes the place of the one showing, so they never stack. Each goes after 5 s (the sender's time when shorter), an urgent one (a red edge) after 10 s, held while the pointer is on it, then the card settles back into the island. A click opens it, a right click clears it. It waits while the launcher is open. Until a notification is opened or cleared, the island shows a bell with a dot; a click on it opens the Notifications page, as do Alt+I and `launcher.notes_key`. `strand toggle notify.open_key` opens the one showing, `notify.clear_key` clears it, and `notify.clear_all_key` clears every one. The page has do not disturb (only urgent ones show; the island's bell is crossed out), Clear all, and every one kept, newest first, across restarts, each row with its summary and the start of its body (a red stripe on an urgent one): Enter opens one (or clears it when it has nothing to open), Delete clears it, Alt+1 to 3 press its buttons, which the selected row lists.
- Screen recording (`strand/record.strand`): records the focused monitor with wf-recorder, every frame Hyprland draws, into `~/Videos/strand-<date>-<time>.mp4`. Start and stop it with "Record screen" in the launcher (or type "rec"), a key bound to `strand toggle record.key`, or by clicking the red dot the island shows while it records. If wf-recorder fails, the launcher row says why.
- Windows VM starting (`strand/vm.strand`): `win-vm` sets each step on the way up (`strand set vm.step "…"`: starting Docker, starting the VM, booting or installing Windows, or connecting to one already up; shutting down and restarting too), and clears it once FreeRDP's window maps (it maps only once the session is up; the RDP probe passes well before) or it has failed. Meanwhile the island's middle, where the wave of what plays goes (the wave gives way), shows a pulsing Windows symbol, the step, each sliding in over the last, and the time so far; a click opens the VM's console in the browser. Only failures and the first-boot install still notify. Once up, its window is tiled (it maps floating and re-floats a tile asked for in its first half second, so `win-vm` asks until it holds), and `win-vm.desktop`'s `StartupWMClass` gives it that entry's icon on the workspace chips. Run without a terminal, it logs to `$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/win-vm.log`.
- Lock (`strand/lock.strand`): answers logind's lock request (`loginctl lock-session`, from Super+L, the launcher, idle and sleep). It grows out of the island over a blurred still of the desktop, is dark with only the island moving, rests round the time and wakes on the first key; a refusal shows PAM's own message. hyprlock is the fallback (`dot_config/hypr/README.md`).
- Admin password prompt (polkit, in `strand/island.strand`): grows out of the island, takes the keyboard and stays until answered or cancelled; a notification waits behind it and the launcher stays shut.
- Monitors (`strand/monitors.strand`, replacing hyprpier): each set of monitors plugged in, known by their make, model and serial whatever ports they are on, gets its default profile from `monitors.toml` (home, work and the laptop alone, made from hyprpier's; a set may have several profiles, one of them its default, and another put up from the Displays page stays up until the set changes or Strand restarts): where each monitor sits, its mode, scale and turn, whether it is on, and which workspaces are its (home: 1 to 5 on the left monitor, 6 to 10 on the right; work: 1 to 4, 5 to 8, 9 and 10; the laptop alone: all ten). A set with no profile gets a best guess, left to right by port with the laptop's panel last, each at its preferred mode and the scale and turn it last had lit (so one turned off and on again comes back as it was), and keeps its workspaces where they are until it is saved. A dock settles for 1.5 s before its layout goes up; then the profile's workspaces move onto their monitors (again until none is left on another, for up to 5 s, since a move can leave Hyprland making a new one where it was), each monitor showing one of its own (after the lid moves, the one it showed before) and the laptop's panel, owning none, an empty one of its own, and the focus stays on the monitor that had it. They move again whenever the set, the monitors on or the profile change, a set not saved in between included (another of the set put up from the Displays page, or the one up forgotten, counts even when its layout is the same; renaming the one up, saving, or adding or forgetting another beside it moves none, so a workspace moved by hand stays), so a restart of Strand moves nothing. While the lid is shut and another monitor is on, the panel is off (the lid read from UPower), at once both ways, also when docking with the lid already shut; the focus stays on the workspace that had it when the lid moved (Hyprland moves it to another monitor), unless that was the panel's. Each layout that takes is written to `~/.config/hypr/monitors.lua` (and Hyprland reloads it): its monitors, the panel always lit so a laptop never starts dark (one saved off is written right of the rest, covering none), and a saved profile's workspace rules, so a workspace made later opens on its monitor and Hyprland starts with the last layout. A lid, or a layout that failed, never reaches it; a saved edit, already up, reaches it at once. The launcher's Displays page (Alt+Y, `launcher.displays_key`) lists the set's profiles, the one up lit and marked "in use", the set's default marked "default" ("Best guess" while the set has none, "edited, not saved" in the accent while an edit is up, the reason in red when Hyprland refused a layout), then its monitors left to right as they sat when the edit began, each with its port, model ("Laptop screen" for the panel), mode, scale, turn and workspaces, "off" or "lid shut", then every other set's profiles, a set's together, each with its monitors by name ("ASUS VS239 ×2 + Laptop", in the order of their ids) and "not connected". A pane beside the list draws the monitors as they sit, in proportion, each with its workspaces (a best guess's too: what saving gives them), the selected one ringed, the panel under a shut lid dim, and under them workspaces 1 to 10 as chips, the selected monitor's lit. A profile selected that isn't up is drawn instead as it is saved (each monitor's place, size, turn, scale and workspaces, lit as saved whatever the lid does now, named by port when it is plugged in, else by model or "Laptop"), to look at only, with its set's monitors under it: "Not in use: Enter puts it up", or "Not connected: it goes up when they are". A profile is only put up and arranged on its own monitors, since every edit is live on them. It is hyprpier's Monitor Arrangement screen, live: each edit goes up at once (Hyprland's monitors move 100 ms after the key) and the layout stays valid: no overlaps, every monitor sharing an edge with the rest (but one ← → stepped past the end of the row beside it, which touches it only at a corner, as hyprpier's did), its top-left at 0,0 and one monitor always lit. With the lid shut the panel isn't laid out with the others: it keeps its place while that still fits beside them (so the lit ones may start right of 0,0, where it waits) and otherwise goes to the right end of the top row, and it doesn't count as the one lit. The edit is a draft over the profile (or the best guess): it never reaches `monitors.lua` or moves a workspace until it is saved, and an edit brought back by hand to what is saved stops being one. Select a monitor with ↑ ↓ or a click on its tile; then (the hint row shows them): ← → move it, past its neighbour in its row (they swap) or, alone in its row, through the stops against the rows next to it: hanging past their left edge, left-aligned, centred and right-aligned with each monitor there, hanging past their right edge; Shift+↑ ↓ stack it, top, centre or bottom against its row's tallest, then on past either end into the row above or below where it stands along it, or a row of its own at the nearest place sharing an edge with the row it left that keeps its place in that row (so ⇧↓ out to a row of its own and ⇧↑ back puts it where it was, before or after a monitor it sits level with as the list of monitors had them, which is hyprpier's order: an edit lists them row by row, and saves them so); Ctrl+R turns it to the next quarter (normal, 90°, 180°, 270°: Hyprland's transform 0 to 3), a flipped one staying flipped; Ctrl+= and Ctrl+− step its scale through 1, 1.25, 1.5, 1.75 and 2, skipping one its mode wouldn't give whole pixels at (Hyprland rounds those: 1.75 at 1920×1080 becomes 1.67, and Strand reports it as refused), the rest re-laid round it; Ctrl+M and Ctrl+Shift+M step its mode down and up through those the monitor offers, largest first, the scale falling back to one that fits (a mode the monitor doesn't list, a custom one, stays put; stepping through a real monitor's several modes is still to be seen on the owner's U2720Q, `monitors-cutover.md` §4, since every test output offers one mode); Alt+1 to Alt+0 give it workspace 1 to 10, taken from the monitor that had it, or take it back off it (each monitor's list kept in order, its lowest the one it shows); Enter turns it off, the rest closing up, or back on at the right end of the top row (never the last one lit; the launcher, if it was on that monitor, comes up on another with the row still selected); the arrange keys show in the hint row while the monitor is lit (one off or under a shut lid takes only Enter and its workspaces); Ctrl+S (or Enter on the profile in use, or on "Best guess") asks the profile's name in place of the search, the one up filled in, the pane showing the layout up (what the save writes) whatever row is selected: the same name saves over it, a new one saves another profile for the set (a set's first is its default) and puts it up, the name of another of the set's profiles asks for Enter again before the layout up is saved over it ("Saved over …"), and a name another set's profile has is refused with a message in the hint row (nothing of another set is overwritten), Esc there cancelling the naming; Esc puts the edit back, the saved layout going up again. The mouse does the same: drag a tile and let go where it should sit (into the row its middle is level with, in order by where it lands, else a row of its own above, between or below the rows at the nearest place sharing an edge; the tile stays within reach of the pane, and one let go over the list changes no row there), drag a workspace chip onto a tile to give it that one, click a chip to give it or take it back. Closing the launcher keeps the edit up, and the profile row still says "edited, not saved" when it opens again: a close by accident loses nothing, and a monitor turned off stays off for the session, as Enter did before the editor. Esc on the page, or a change in the set of monitors, puts it back (a change of set says so in a notification). The list keeps the order the monitors had when the edit began, so the selection stays put while the pane shows the new order. hyprpier's "remove monitor" is Enter (off) here: a profile belongs to one set of monitors, so one not plugged in isn't in it, and one plugged in but unwanted is saved off. On a profile, connected or not (the hint row shows its keys): Enter on another of the set puts it up at once, its workspaces placed again as for a new set and an edit not saved put back (the set's default comes up again the next time the set connects); F2 renames it in place of the search, as the Thunderbolt page names a dock (a name another profile has is refused); Del forgets it (the set's first left becomes its default if it was; the one up forgotten, the set's default or the best guess goes up); Ctrl+D makes it its set's default, the one up staying up. Enter on one not connected only says it isn't. A click on a profile's row selects it, its layout in the pane; a click on the one selected does what Enter does (on one not connected, nothing). `monitors.toml` holds a `[[profiles]]` table a profile, in the order saved: its `name` (no two alike, whatever the set), `setup` (its set's key, the monitors' ids sorted and joined by " + ") and `default`, then its monitors as `[[profiles.mons]]`. A list, not tables keyed by name or by set: Strand writes a list element changed in a field in place, so a rename or a new default is a one-line diff (two for a new default: the old one's line too), and the comment over a profile stays with it, where a key renamed in a table would move to the end of the file without its comment; the page keeps the names unique. A file still in the old shape (`[profiles."<set>"]` with a `name`, one a set) doesn't load: Strand says so in its log and `strand watch`, runs the best guess and saves nothing over it; the commit that brought several profiles a set converted the owner's file, nothing lost. `monitors.toml` lives in chezmoi's source (`state/monitors.toml`, linked from `~/.config/strand/`), so a save is a change to the repo, committed with chezmoi's autoCommit at the next `chezmoi` write or by hand. Arrange monitors on Alt+Y; editing the file by hand is only a fallback. Trade-off: the workspace rules are Hyprland's own, so Hyprland puts a workspace moved by hand back on its monitor when it reloads (theme-switch reloads it) or a monitor comes or goes.
- Thunderbolt (`launcher.strand`, through bolt): the launcher's Thunderbolt page (Alt+H, `launcher.bolt_key`, listed while a dock or other device is plugged in or stored) lists bolt's devices with their names, a waiting one first: "wants to connect" in orange, then each with its state and how it is allowed ("connected · always allowed", "allowed by hand", "this time"; "not plugged in"). A device plugged in that bolt has not let in opens the launcher here a second later, once each time it is plugged in, like Bluetooth's pairing: Enter allows it always (bolt enrolls it, policy auto); Shift+Enter allows it this once; Esc leaves it blocked and closes. A stored device with policy manual is let in with Enter. Del forgets a stored device (bolt asks again at its next plug; it stays in for now), and F2 names it, the name asked in place of the search. `~/.local/bin/bolt-devices` reads bolt's devices as a JSON line on each change for the page; bolt's own methods enroll and forget, `boltctl` authorizes once and names. After a suspend, `thunderbolt-resume.service` (installed by `run_onchange_after_setup-thunderbolt-resume.sh`) resets the Thunderbolt controllers, which wake wedged on this laptop, then unplugs and replugs in software each device bolt has let in, so a dock's USB hubs come back; one not let in stays out, and the page asks for it.
- Keep awake: a switch on the launcher's Power page that holds off the idle lock (`inhibit_idle`), shown as a cup on the island, which a click turns off.
